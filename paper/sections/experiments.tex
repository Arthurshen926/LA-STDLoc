\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Protocol}

We freeze one method configuration across scenes. Mapping images are used for
Gaussian-prior construction and all offline localization-map learning; official
test images are used only for evaluation. The deployment frontend uses native
SuperPoint, full-resolution coordinates with the explicit pixel-center
convention, global cosine top-1 matching, and one PoseLib PnP/RANSAC solve.

We report median, mean, and 90th-percentile translation and rotation error;
recall at $5\,$cm/$5^\circ$; raw and RANSAC-inlier correspondence precision at
2 and 4 pixels; catastrophic failures; primitive, track, and final anchor
counts; map memory; build time; and localization P50/P90 latency.

\subsection{Datasets and Baselines}

The evaluation covers five Cambridge Landmarks scenes, all seven 7Scenes
scenes, and all twelve 12Scenes scenes. The indoor main tables use the same
off-the-shelf prior: the official vanilla 2DGS training recipe applied to the
mapping RGB images and the published SfM pseudo-ground-truth (pGT) camera
registries~\cite{brachmann2021pgt}, without semantic masks, depth supervision,
localization features, or task-specific Gaussian pruning. We import camera
poses and the official split, but discard the reference model's 3D points and
feature observations. A new mapping-only SfM triangulation initializes the RGB
Gaussian prior. The prior and every localization-map artifact therefore
exclude test images. Table~\ref{tab:indoor_protocol} summarizes the frozen
indoor protocol. Results based on the original D-SLAM poses are treated as a
separate protocol and are not mixed into the main tables.

A representative prior-flexibility subset compares vanilla 3DGS, vanilla
2DGS, an enhanced 2DGS prior, and a feed-forward AnySplat prior while keeping
every localization-map stage fixed.

Our principal comparison is between the Gaussian-supported KCS/GWFF bootstrap
(A0), defined as the 48K Stage-A scaffold with the identity descriptor metric,
and the complete compact reconstructed map (A1), which uses the learned shared
metric. Both use exactly the same full-resolution native SuperPoint frontend,
uncapped global top-1 correspondences, and PoseLib settings. We evaluate each
fixed map with RANSAC seeds 2026, 2027, and 2028 and report their distribution,
rather than selecting a seed per scene. Track-only and base-only maps are
reported on representative scenes to isolate track identity and Gaussian
coverage.

\subsection{Analysis Plan}

The evaluation tests four questions: whether A1 consistently improves A0;
whether compact topology preserves both central and tail pose accuracy; whether
the reconstructed map is robust to off-the-shelf prior quality; and whether the
offline cost yields a simpler online pipeline. A scene failure is attributed
only after auditing dataset convention, prior reconstruction, track coverage,
and candidate precision under the frozen protocol.
