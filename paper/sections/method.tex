\section{Method}

\subsection{Evidence-Grounded Anchor Registry}

Let a frozen RGB Gaussian prior be
$\Gprior=\{(\boldsymbol\mu_j,\boldsymbol\Sigma_j,
\alpha_j,\mathbf{c}_j)\}_{j=1}^{N_G}$ and let the mapping set
$\Queries_m=\{(I_q,K_q,T_q)\}$ contain RGB images with calibrated poses. We
reconstruct a single Evidence-Grounded Anchor Registry
\begin{equation}
  \Anchors=\{a_i\}_{i=1}^{N_A},\qquad
  a_i=(\iota_i,\mathcal O_i,\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf\Sigma_i,
  \mathbf d_i,E_i^{\rm surf},E_i^{\rm vis},L_i,r_i).
\end{equation}
Here $\iota_i$ is an observation-grounded identity,
$\mathcal O_i=\{(q,k)\}$ indexes its real mapping observations,
$(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf\Sigma_i)$ is its one PnP point and uncertainty,
$\mathbf d_i$ is its one deployment descriptor, $E_i^{\rm surf}$ and
$E_i^{\rm vis}$ are surface and raster-visibility evidence, $L_i$ is Gaussian
source lineage, and $r_i$ records why the selector admitted the row. These
fields are orthogonal: one primitive may support multiple Anchors and one
Anchor may retain multiple primitive sources. Thus
\begin{equation}
  \text{Gaussian ID}\neq\text{observation-track ID}\neq\text{Anchor ID}.
\end{equation}
In particular, Gaussian support does not create a second localization identity.
The legacy \texttt{anchor\_type} field is serialized for artifact compatibility
only; it is construction provenance rather than our representation.

\subsection{Mapping Evidence and Wide Bootstrap}

Native SuperPoint observations are extracted from each mapping image. Detector
sampling, NMS, processed resolution, and the cross-view pair graph form a
mapping-evidence contract. A change to this contract regenerates all descendant
tracks, geometry, graphs, teachers, topology, and metric state; it is not a new
deployed Anchor class. Detector-density and pair-policy variants are controlled
acquisition factors, not accuracy contributions claimed by this formulation.

Keypoint-consensus sampling (KCS) selects a broad Gaussian-supported scaffold:
a primitive must receive support from multiple views, viewpoint bins, and
trajectory bins. Geometry-weighted feature fusion (GWFF) initializes each
selected descriptor from visible observations with robust cosine trimming. The
48K setting is a safety cap, not a required map size; adaptive construction
stops at consensus saturation.

The wide scaffold then localizes every mapping view with the same global
retrieval rule used at deployment. Known mapping poses label keep, swap, miss,
and false-attractor outcomes. Stage-A reconstruction uses
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{wide}} =
  \lambda_k\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{keep}}+
  \lambda_s\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{swap}}+
  \lambda_m\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{miss}}+
  \lambda_a\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{attractor}}+
  \lambda_l\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{local}}+
  \lambda_t\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{trust}}.
\end{equation}
Protected precise correspondences and local peaks bound the update. Its length
is calibrated from mapping-query epochs,
$S_A=\lceil E_A|\Queries_m|\rceil$, rather than fixed across datasets. This A0
state supplies the descriptors used to construct observation evidence.

\subsection{Observation Identity and Geometry Materialization}

We form reciprocal, cycle-consistent cross-view associations before assigning
Gaussian lineage. A track $t\in\Tracks$ is accepted only when its observations
span sufficient views and viewpoint bins. Its image-space estimate is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}_{t}^{\rm img}=
  \arg\min_{\mathbf{x}}\sum_{(q,\mathbf{u})\in t}
  \rho\!\left(\left\|\pi(K_q,T_q,\mathbf{x})-
  \mathbf{u}\right\|_2\right),
\end{equation}
subject to parallax, reprojection, and covariance gates. Gaussian
rasterization then contributes surface support, visibility, composition mass,
and primitive lineage without replacing this identity.

Heterogeneous geometry evidence crosses one materialization boundary,
\begin{equation}
 (\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf\Sigma_i,g_i,s_i)=
 \operatorname{MaterializeGeometry}(e_i^{\rm img},e_i^{\rm surf}),
\end{equation}
where $g_i$ records image-triangulated, surface-regularized, or
surface-initialized provenance and $s_i$ records actual surface dependence.
An image-stable track keeps $\mathbf{x}_{t}^{\rm img}$; a weak track may use a
surface-regularized estimate only after an upstream mapping gate accepts it;
and a surface-initialized fallback retains its existing surface point. Surface
evidence availability is stored separately from surface dependence. In the
validated Heads, Stairs, and ShopFacade artifacts, no deployed Track uses the
accepted surface-regularized mode. We therefore claim image-triangulated rows
plus selected surface-initialized fallback rows, not a demonstrated adaptive
geometry-fusion gain.

\subsection{Precision Core and Sufficiency Completion}

All eligible identities enter one candidate registry and one incremental
selected state. The selector has two conceptual phases. First, the
\emph{Precision Core} accepts quality-ranked cross-view candidates until the
mapping-query p10 matching-rank target is reached or an eligibility cap is
exhausted. Second, \emph{Sufficiency Completion} draws from the unified
leftover registry to improve two sufficiency criteria: bipartite matching rank
and pose observability. The organizing objective is a smallest sufficient map,
\begin{equation}
 \min_{S\subseteq\Anchors}|S|\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
 R_q(S)\ge m_q,\qquad H_q(S)\succeq h_q I,
 \quad q\in\Queries_m,
\end{equation}
under frozen eligibility, reliability, diversity, and resource constraints.
Our compatibility implementation does not claim to solve or certify these
per-query constraints exactly. It first uses a mapping-query p10 rank target,
then feasible per-query matching completion, and finally a budgeted greedy
full-SE(3) objective with marginal-gain stopping. Thus the equation states the
design goal, whereas the serialized selection trace records the constraints
actually enforced.

Matching completion maintains a query-row/Anchor bipartite state with unit
query-row capacity and augmenting-path reassignment. Observability completion
uses dynamic full-SE(3) D-optimal gain together with image, depth, and spatial
diversity; its information matrix is updated after every addition, while
source lineage and spatial voxel capacities remain separate. Every selected
row receives exactly one primary reason: \texttt{precision},
\texttt{matching\_completion}, or \texttt{observability\_completion}. These are
reasons in one map, not Track/Gaussian or Core/Reserve deployment branches. The
standard monotone-submodular guarantee applies only to the
cardinality-constrained D-optimal term, not the complete constrained selector.

\subsection{Bounded Shared-Metric Refresh}

On the fixed compact Registry, the A1 stage learns one bounded low-rank metric
shared by query and Anchor descriptors. Complete-positive retrieval,
current-map hard outcomes, protected correspondences, and trajectory-group DRO
train the update. Its mapping-only duration is
\begin{equation}
  S_C=\left\lceil E_C|\Queries_m|\right\rceil,
\end{equation}
so checkpoint steps are scene-calibrated rather than a universal constant.
Geometry, RGB appearance, identity, and topology remain frozen. The deployed
map retains one vector per Anchor; no prototype family, dense refinement, or
test-time context branch is required.

\subsection{Sparse Deployment}

For a test image, native SuperPoint runs once. The shared metric transforms
query and map descriptors, global cosine top-1 gives one Anchor correspondence
per query descriptor, and all correspondences enter one standard PoseLib
PnP/RANSAC call. There is no landmark-side match cap, learned test-time
selector, rendering stage, or second pose solve. Grid-index keypoints receive a
fixed $+0.5$ pixel-center offset before PnP. Detector density follows processed
image area, while focal/angular and metric-space thresholds are resolved only
from mapping data. Test queries never enter evidence construction, selection,
metric learning, or calibration.
